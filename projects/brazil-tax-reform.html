A new tax system, and no view of what it will cost

Brazil is replacing five consumption taxes (PIS, Cofins, IPI, ICMS and ISS) with a dual VAT, IBS and CBS, phased in from 2026 to 2033. Two changes hit cash directly. With split payment, the tax is taken out at the moment the customer pays, so the company loses the money it used to hold until the tax was due. And a company only earns credit on its purchases once the supplier's tax has actually been paid.

Finance leaders have to decide prices and contracts before the final rates are even published, and their ERP cannot show them the size of the impact.

Turn existing ledger data into tax decisions

The goal was to read the tax already recorded in the SAP ledger and turn it into answers a CFO can act on: the effective rate today against the reform, which costs will earn credit, how much float split payment takes away, and what that means for prices. It had to reuse the data and model the company already had, not start a new system.

Extend the ledger model and encode the law as data

  • Extended an existing trial-balance model with four new objects for tax lines, rates, scenarios and obligations, instead of building a parallel one. Only four extra SAP columns were needed.
  • Encoded the reform's rules as parameter tables: the 2026–2033 transition, state and interstate rates, and credit rules. Switching a scenario recalculates everything without touching code.
  • Built a synthetic SAP dataset for three Brazilian companies over 24 months, with electronic invoices, plus a validator, so the model could be tested end to end before touching client data.
  • Designed the views a CFO uses: a cockpit, a month-by-month cash simulation of today's tax against the reform, a credit map, and a split-payment radar.

A working prototype that matches an independent calculation

Deployed to a sandbox environment, the model produced the same totals as an independent offline calculation, tax type by tax type and cent by cent. The views and KPIs passed platform validation with zero errors, and I checked the legal claims behind the rules against their sources.

This is a prototype running on synthetic data. The next phases are refunds, tax classification of real items, and a rollout on real company data.
